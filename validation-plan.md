# Validation test plan

## Scope and hypotheses

This is a proposed class alpha study for students currently studying the biology content in Lectures 19–22, beginning with DNA synthesis and primers. Use participants with prior classroom exposure to those concepts. Record eligibility and recruitment method. Participation is voluntary, unrelated to grades, and uses anonymous participant codes. Ask participants to avoid entering personal information in free text. Use team-authored/reviewed review material.

The earlier pitch proposed roughly 60 students over three weeks, 50% completing a topic every week, 40% of sessions more than three days before an exam, and a 10-percentage-point concept-check advantage. A short alpha test cannot establish those claims. This protocol supplies narrower usability and learning signals. Keep the original KPIs marked untested unless the longer study is actually conducted. Record the subject change from ECON 2010 to biology in the submission.

All thresholds below are proposed team decision rules, not published benchmarks or measured results. Agree on them before recruiting and report any deviations.

H1 — Students can complete topic selection, playback, a question, a follow-up, and a return home without assistance. Proposed support threshold is at least 80% task completion without moderator intervention and no unresolved blocker preventing a core step.

H2 — The video review package yields a larger short-term score gain than equivalent written review. Proposed exploratory target is a 10-percentage-point difference in mean gains. This is a directional alpha signal. Small convenience samples do not establish a reliable causal effect or broader generalization.

H3 — Students choose to return outside the supervised session. Offer access for seven days with no grade incentive. Proposed support threshold is at least 50% of assigned Clipcademy participants completing a review on a later calendar day without an individual reminder. This measures short-term return. The original multiweek/cramming hypothesis remains untested.

## Recruitment and allocation

Aim for 12 eligible volunteers, six per condition, as a feasible class target. This is not a power-derived sample size. Report actual counts, prior familiarity, withdrawals, and missing assessments. If recruitment falls short, report the study as incomplete and show descriptive findings.

Prepare six A and six B labels, shuffle them, and allocate in enrollment order after consent and the pre-check. Keep the assessor blind to condition where feasible. A receives Clipcademy. B receives the same approved explanations as text with identical practice questions, feedback, and branching. Both receive the same ten-minute maximum study opportunity. Record actual study time. The comparison estimates the incremental value of the video format within this particular package, not the package against usual studying. Explain this change from the original pitch.

## Session procedure

1. Explain the task, voluntary participation, data collected, and withdrawal option. Assign a participant code and confirm prior exposure.
2. Administer pre-check Form A, three points, without feedback. Record score.
3. Reveal the randomly assigned condition and give the same study instructions and ten-minute opportunity.
4. Record first-answer behavior naturally. Do not force an incorrect answer during the learning comparison. Participants who answer correctly may never encounter remediation.
5. Administer post-check Form B, three points, without feedback. Use the scoring rubric and record score and elapsed time.
6. After the post-check, ask all A participants to complete the scripted follow-up usability task by deliberately choosing a specified wrong answer. This occurs after outcome measurement so it does not alter the measured learning intervention.
7. Ask the interview questions and record assistance, confusion, and verbatim comments only with permission.
8. Give A access for seven days. Do not individually prompt return. Offer B access after the comparison measurements are complete. Ask both groups about other studying before delayed assessment.
9. At 48–72 hours, administer a new parallel three-point delayed check if feasible. Keep it separate from the primary immediate result. Record missingness rather than entering zeros.

For an unusable prototype, first conduct a small usability rehearsal and fix blockers. Document changes, then freeze a version for the learning comparison. Do not mix data across changing builds without disclosure.

## Metrics and calculations

| Metric | Calculation | Required reporting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unassisted task completion | A participants completing all scripted steps without help / A participants starting the task × 100 | Numerator, denominator, and assistance definition |
| Score percent | Points earned / 3 × 100 | Raw points and percent |
| Individual gain | Post-check percent − pre-check percent | Keep paired participant records |
| Format gain difference | Mean gain in A − mean gain in B | Group sizes, individual gains, group means, and uncertainty if assessed |
| Later-day return | A participants completing a review on a later day without an individual reminder / all assigned A participants × 100 | Exact return timestamps and reminder status |
| Delayed gain | Delayed percent − pre-check percent | Actual follow-up interval and missing counts |
| Content defects | Reviewed items with an error / all independently audited items × 100 | Inspect all released demo items, severity, and corrections |

With six A participants, 80% requires at least five completing the task since 5/6 = 83.3%. A 50% return target requires at least three since 3/6 = 50%. These are threshold examples, not observed results.

Do not discard participants who struggled or failed to return. Describe assigned groups, paired-score analysis counts, withdrawals, and incomplete records separately. Discuss baseline differences, small sample, convenience sampling, prior exposure, short assessments, study-time differences, and the fact that the three-question check is not a standardized learning measure.

## Decision and submission

Report each hypothesis separately. Mark a hypothesis supported under the stated alpha rule only when its planned data and threshold are met. Otherwise mark not supported or insufficient evidence. If the rubric requires Validated/Invalidated/Pivot, map supported to 'Validated for this alpha sample', not supported to 'Invalidated under this test', and disclose incomplete evidence explicitly rather than inventing a result.

Possible actions depend on observed findings. Navigation failures lead to interface changes. Accurate use without a learning advantage leads to examining lesson/question design or video value. Low voluntary return leads to revisiting the between-class use case. Content errors block release and require content review. Explain any pivot with the actual findings and state that its new direction remains untested.

Submit the frozen version ID, participant counts, allocation method, raw anonymized results, metric calculations, qualitative themes, limitations, and the decision. Use decision-report-template.md.
